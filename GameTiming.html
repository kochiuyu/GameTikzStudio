Academic Context

Sequential timelines describe the timing of the game in dynamic economic models. They specify the exact chronological order in which information is revealed, beliefs are updated via Bayes' rule, and commitments are locked in before terminal payoffs are distributed.

📖 Understanding Timing TikZ Code

  • \def\dx{3.6}: Defines the uniform horizontal distance in centimeters between consecutive stage marks.
  • \draw[very thick, ->]: Draws the continuous timeline arrow representing time progression.
  • (T0, T1, ...): Text nodes placed below the axis ticks anchoring time intervals ($t=0, 1\dots$).
  • (P0, P1, ...): Badges showing the acting player or Nature whose turn it is to move.
  • (A0, A1, ...): Boxed actions and strategic commitments made during each stage.
  • decorations.pathreplacing: Produces the top grouping brackets labeling macroeconomic/game phases.

Dynamic Game Timing & Stages 101

How economists communicate multi-period sequential interactions

1. Why Use a Timing Diagram?

When a game has continuous actions (like price, quantity $q$, or education years $e$) or complex multi-step protocols (contracting, signaling, bargaining), drawing a full game tree with infinite branches is impractical. A Timing Diagram summarizes the exact chronological order of moves along a horizontal time axis ($t = 0, 1, 2\dots$).

2. Anatomy of a Stage ($t$)

  • Mover Pill (Top): Which entity makes a choice (e.g. Nature, Principal, Worker, Firm).
  • Action Description: What variables are chosen or what contract is proposed.
  • Information State / Beliefs (Below Axis): What is publicly known versus what remains private information, plus any Bayesian belief updates.
  • Payoffs: When and how utility is realized.

3. Asymmetric Information & Nature ($t=0$)

In Bayesian games, Nature ($\mathcal{N}$) moves first at $t = 0$ to assign a player's private type (e.g., whether a borrower is trustworthy, or whether an employee is high ability). The informed player then acts, transmitting signals or choosing actions that uninformed observers analyze.
